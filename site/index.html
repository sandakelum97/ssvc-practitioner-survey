    Full details

    Who. A master's thesis by (), supervised by .

    Why. SSVC (Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization), from the CERT Coordination Center, decides patch priority partly from facts about the organisation's own environment, such as how exposed a system is and how much harm its failure would cause. These are usually filled in by hand. The research asks whether they can be derived from data a managed security provider already holds, and compares that with what experienced practitioners decide.

    The scenarios. The organisations and assets are fictional, built to resemble real sectors. The vulnerabilities are real, published ones, described as they were known at the time.

    What is collected. Four answers about your experience (years in the field, role, whether you work at an MSSP, whether you have used SSVC), your ratings for each scenario, and the time each answer was saved. There are no free-text boxes.

    Where. The database is hosted by Supabase in ; the page is hosted by Vercel. Both keep technical request logs, including IP addresses, under their own privacy policies (Supabase, Vercel). The researcher does not collect or use IP addresses. Your browser keeps your place in the survey on this device only, so you can reload or come back later.

    Reporting. Results are reported as summaries. No group smaller than five people is reported by its background answers. Raw answers are deleted by ; summary results remain in the thesis.

    Withdrawing. Because nothing identifies you, the completion code is the only way to find your answers. Email it to by .
