    How practitioners prioritise vulnerabilities

    You will read 23 short scenarios, each an asset in an organisation with a vulnerability on it, and say how you would judge it. It takes about minutes.

    Why this survey is being done

    Organisations cannot fix every vulnerability at once, so they decide which to fix first. SSVC (Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization), a method from the CERT Coordination Center, makes that decision partly from facts about the organisation's own environment: how exposed the system is, and how much harm its failure would cause. Today those facts are usually filled in by hand.

    This research asks whether they can be worked out automatically, from data a managed security provider already holds. To judge that fairly, it needs to know what experienced practitioners would decide in the same situations. Your ratings are that benchmark. There are no right or wrong answers; your professional judgement is what counts.

    The organisations and assets in the scenarios are fictional, built to resemble real sectors. The vulnerabilities are real, published ones, described as they were known at the time.

    How to rate each scenario

    Each scenario describes one asset in one organisation, and one vulnerability on it, as it was known at the time. Judge it as if this organisation were your responsibility.

    • Rate three things: the asset's System Exposure, the Human Impact if the vulnerability were exploited, and the action you would take. Then say how confident you are.
    • Please don't look anything up. Use only what the scenario tells you and your own experience.
    • Each answer is saved when you continue, and can't be changed afterwards.
